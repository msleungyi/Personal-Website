abstract: |
  This dissertation presents tools and methodological approaches for enhancing conceptual clarity and understanding methodological heterogeneity for facilitating replicability in developmental dyslexia research and psychological science. It comprises three empirical studies that address conceptual and terminological variability, task design heterogeneity in psycholinguistic testing, and methodological variability in research practices.

  The first study (Chapter 2) addresses inconsistencies in definitions of terms related to psychology and metascience. It introduces “Re-SearchTerms”, a Shiny app that allows researchers to explore and compare 2211 definitions of 261 terms from academic texts and crowdsourced glossaries. Using natural language processing techniques, the app visualises semantic relationships of the wording of the selected terms’ definitions. This tool supports more deliberate and accurate use of terminology, especially in interdisciplinary contexts where definitional ambiguity hinders communication.

  The second study (Chapter 3) investigated how task format, reading comprehension, and linguistic item features affect item difficulty in morphological awareness (MA) assessments. One hundred and two adult L1 and L2 English speakers completed four pseudoword-based MA tasks—word analogy, picture, single sentence, and short text—alongside a standardised reading comprehension test. Task type and reading comprehension both significantly influenced item difficulty. Linguistic features such as morpheme type and spelling cues also predicted item difficulty, while orthographic shifts had no significant effect. These findings emphasise the importance of aligning item design with the intended goal of assessment—whether to capture detailed profiles or differentiate individuals based on the intended construct.

  The third study (Chapter 4) was a systematic review of empirical studies on the subtyping of developmental dyslexia (screened items = 1362 entries) to examine the methodological heterogeneity in dyslexia subtyping methods. The review found wide variation in theoretical models, data pre-processing steps, statistical techniques, and validation methods. To support the review of the existing subtyping methods, we developed a Shiny app to visualise the heterogeneity in the existing pipelines of subtyping methods. This work highlights the need for clearer reporting and greater scrutiny of analytic choices in subtyping research in psychology and biomedicine.

  Collectively, this dissertation highlights the critical need for greater theoretical and methodological specificity not only in dyslexia research but also in psychological science more broadly. By addressing challenges in terminological consistency, task validity, and methodological variances in research, it contributes to understanding different sources of heterogeneity that influence replicability in dyslexia research and beyond. It lays the groundwork for promoting tools and methodological approaches that support the synthesis of scientific knowledge, with the aim of enhancing replicability both within dyslexia research and across psychological science.
